### [Java4Shell: Reported RCE in OpenJDK Base64 Processing](https://calif.io/java4shell/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Calif.io has reported a remote code execution vulnerability, dubbed Java4Shell, in OpenJDK&\#x27;s Base64 processing, with the write-up describing a network-based heap-grooming technique \(&quot;Remote Heap Feng Shui&quot;\) that is sensitive to round-trip time and jitter and was tested over public IP addresses rather than only in a lab. The supplied excerpt does not include full technical details, a CVE assignment, or confirmed patch status, so the vulnerability should be treated as a vendor-reported disclosure rather than a fully verified, patched finding. Community discussion indicates the flaw affects HotSpot-based JVMs, while non-HotSpot implementations are reportedly unaffected.

hackernews · werrett · Nov 17, 21:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50538240)

**「Why Base64 parsing matters here」** Base64 decoding is a common, often untrusted-input path in Java applications, and HotSpot&\#x27;s JIT compiler generates optimized stubs for such routines, which is why the discussion centers on HotSpot-specific code generation rather than the JVM language specification itself. The exploit reportedly depends on that JIT-generated stub, which is why implementations that do not use HotSpot&\#x27;s compiler are described as unaffected.

**「Who is affected」** According to community discussion, the affected set is any HotSpot VM build, including OpenJDK builds, Oracle JDK, and GraalVM JDK, while OpenJ9, GCJ, and Azul Prime \(Zing\), which replaces C2 with its own JIT, are described as unaffected. Semgrep has published free detection scripts that check for the relevant conditions, and one commenter reports an unconfirmed workaround of running Java with the -XX:UseAVX=2 flag, which should be treated as unverified until confirmed by the vendor.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters describe the work as impressive but note the exploit conditions are very specific even though many hosts remain impacted, and one commenter predicts a serious compromise of critical infrastructure within six months, an opinion rather than an established fact. A separate commenter offers an unconfirmed claim, attributed to posts on X, that the vulnerability relates to the AVX512 implementation of Base64 decoding and can be worked around with -XX:UseAVX=2, which is not independently verified in the supplied material.

OpenAI introduced GPT-6.2 Astra, its frontier model for hard projects, and GPT-6.2 Sol, an efficient model for everyday work, both available in the API as \`gpt-6.2-astra\` and \`gpt-6.2-sol\`. Astra is priced at $8/MTok input and $40/MTok output, 80% of the prior 6 Astra price of $10/$50, with cached input at $0.40/million versus $1/million before. Sol holds at $2/$10, unchanged from 6.1 Sol and 6 Sol and half the price of GPT-5.6 Sol at $4/$20. Simon Willison shipped LLM 0.38 with support for the new models.

hackernews · jasondavies · Nov 17, 17:46 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50535230)

**「Predecessor models and pricing」** GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol are minor-version successors to OpenAI&\#x27;s existing GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol lines, so the release reads as a price and speed update rather than a new architecture. Astra&\#x27;s $8/$40 per million input/output tokens is 80% of GPT-6 Astra&\#x27;s $10/$50, with cached reads cut from $1 to $0.40 per million tokens, while Sol holds at $2/$10 — half the $4/$20 charged for GPT-5.6 Sol. Both models are available in the API at launch with an &quot;Ultrafast&quot; tier.

**「Cost and speed for agentic work」** The steeper cached-input discount \($0.40 versus $1 per million tokens\) directly lowers the cost of long-running agentic sessions, and one commenter reported Astra&\#x27;s cost on a data analytics exam falling from about $20 to about $4 while Sol dropped from $2.6 to $1.2, with similar accuracy and faster runtimes. Developers should note the accuracy gains came partly from fewer tool calls, and the same commenter flagged that the models sometimes &quot;finished early&quot; without finding the expected answer.

**「Mixed evaluations」** Commenters report strong results on some tasks, with one team ranking Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 as their top two models on a parametric CAD harness and calling them faster and cheaper than their 6.1 counterparts. Others push back on office-style work, with one user calling the Slideshow, Workbook, and memo examples &quot;particularly awful&quot; and worse than what they got from GPT-5.6 Sol, while Theo Browne&\#x27;s pre-release feedback letter complained that Astra can block on long tasks by asking users about internal implementation details they cannot act on.
